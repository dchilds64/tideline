# Visual direction

Graphical realism and immersion are high priorities for Tideline, as requested by the project owner on November 5, 2026. Preserve the complete, reliable single-player rescue mission while improving the visual experience.

Prioritize convincing materials at physical scale, natural silhouettes, human proportions and animation, plausible light sources and shadows, coherent environmental detail, and restrained interface/effects. Do not equate darkness, blur, excessive bloom, or high polygon counts with realism. Keep doorways, actors, cover and interaction prompts readable at night. Use the optional minimal HUD to reduce clutter without removing mission access.

The current renderer combines original environment geometry, locally bundled CC0 scanned surface maps, a credited textured/skinned tactical character for teammates and armed enemies, and detailed first-person rifle, optic and gloved-arm assets. Operators use retargeted authored animation with grip and stationary foot constraints. First-person presentation adds a physical sight aligned with shots, camera-relative shoulder anchoring, a native support-arm skeleton and a timed magazine reload. The earlier fitted hands were rejected for malformed anatomy; the replacement preserves native fingers and uses a fixed trigger-hand pose. Structural attachment tests do not prove convincing anatomy: inspect ready, ADS and intermediate reload poses in clear lighting. Finger contact and mechanical handling still need polish; the magazine swap is offscreen and near-wall weapon collision remains simplified. The largest remaining visual gaps are the procedural civilian hostage, character variation and animation polish, convincing vegetation, and more varied architecture. Future work should favor those assets over adding map area or more simulation features.

Retain an offline runtime, documented asset provenance, an accessible graphics fallback, and browser visual verification. Do not introduce decorative geometry that appears to provide cover without matching gameplay collision. Existing small surface props are decorative; cover and wall geometry continue to come from the shared world model.
